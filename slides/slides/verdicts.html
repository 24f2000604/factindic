Verdicts

H1, not supported. Tokens per word do not explain hallucination.

H2, partly supported. Romanized input costs 0.18 precision in Hindi and 0.43 in Gujarati with B1; mixing in English nouns costs nothing measurable.

H4, not supported. Anchor-aware decoding does not beat simpler decoding.

Pipeline. All four stages are not measurably more precise than a prompt with an anchor checklist, at ten times the time; its verifier is the weak link.

Second run. Other hardware, older code: same verdicts on H2 and H4.

Future work: H3, the verification router against human labels; and Romanized input itself.

Code and results: github.com/aaryavrdhanpandey-star/factindic

Close with the practical advice: for a small open model, list the source’s names, numbers and dates in the prompt, and convert Romanized input carefully before anything else. Then take questions.
