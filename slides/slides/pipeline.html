The pipeline: four stages around one small model

Stage 1

Anchors

Normalize the text. Find names, numbers and dates, with keys that match across scripts.

Stage 2

Prompt

Gemma-4 E2B with factuality rules and the anchor checklist (prompt B1, chosen on dev data).

Stage 3

Decode

One candidate from anchor-aware contrastive decoding, plus four samples.

Stage 4

Verify

Check each sentence, pick the best candidate, remove unsupported sentences once.

Verification: if a claim’s names and numbers survive translation, MiniCheck scores the English; otherwise a native-language NLI model scores the original.

Everything runs on one 8 GB laptop GPU with open models; no paid service was used in the experiments

The summary is always in the native script of the matrix language. Gemma-4 became the backbone because H1 failed and Sarvam-1 hallucinated 18 points more. Contrastive decoding means: prefer tokens that the document makes more likely.
